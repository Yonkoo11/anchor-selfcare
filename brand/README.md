# ANCHOR brand mark

Made with the Logo Forge Kit pipeline (Understand → Concept → Route → Generate in rounds →
Critique → Finalize). `BRAND-TRUTH.md` and `ART-DIRECTION.md` are the stage 0 and 1 artifacts.
`round1/`, `round2/` and `round3/` are the paper trail, kept so the reasoning is checkable.

## The mark

A barbless anchor. Solid head, wide crossbar, shallow open cradle, round caps throughout.

The kit says to name and forbid the lazy idea, and for a product called ANCHOR that is the
nautical anchor. So it was forbidden, and two full rounds went to abstract alternatives:
a damped wave settling onto a point, a plumb, a weighted ring, a waterline, a cradle. Every
one of them collided with a glyph that already means something else. The wave read as an EKG
trace, and its flattened tail read as flatlining, which is an awful thing to put on a mental
health product. The plumb was literally a letter T. The stack read as water, or as a hamburger
menu at small size. The dot on a bar was the default empty-avatar glyph. The cradle was a
smiley face. The waterline was a sunrise, which the art direction bans as a wellness cliché.

The literal mark won on evidence. The kit's anti-literal rule is aimed at category clichés,
a magnifying glass for a search app, and this is a name-mark for a brand actually named
Anchor with a ticker to match. Recognition beats abstraction at this size.

What keeps it from being stock: **no barbs and no ring.** A stock anchor has spiked flukes
and an open shackle. This one has a shallow open cradle and a solid head. It holds, it does
not grip. That matches a product whose whole argument is that the smallest thing is enough.

## Palette

Field sage `#7D8B7A` into moss `#6B7B6A`. Mark cream `#FAF8F5`. One accent, nothing else.

## Legibility, tested not assumed

Rendered and inspected at 400, 128, 48, 32 and 16px with a circular crop, the sizes X and
browser tabs actually use. It holds at 16px; the head stays a distinct dot and the cradle
stays open. Proof sheet: `proof-sizes.png`.

## Caveats

Authored as vector and rendered natively at every size. Nothing here was upscaled and no
image model was used, so there are no generation artifacts. The header is the one piece
with type in it; it uses Georgia, which matches the site's serif stack.
